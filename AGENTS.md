## The project in five lines

- **IMR Coral ID** is a static web app (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, GitHub Pages) live at <https://ppericard.github.io/coral-id-imr/>.
- It teaches the **39 coral genera on the IMR slate** (Institute for Marine Research, Dauin, Philippines): 34 numbered genera that IMR surveys track, plus 5 starred look-alikes (Duncanopsammia, Alveopora, Merulina, Cyphastrea, Halomitra) that are not tracked but must be told apart; and, since 2026-11-02, **Millepora** (fire coral: not a hard coral, so not on the IMR coral list, but on the IMR benthic slate and very common at Dauin and in ReefCloud photos), which counts like them: 40 genera in the games and the progress (`slateGenera()` in `app.js`).
- Owner: **Pierre Pericard** (GitHub `ppericard`), Research Fellow at IMR since October 2026. Users: Pierre and the Research Assistants (RAs) he trains, most of them on phones.
- RAs' first job is genus-level labelling of top-down benthic photos in **ReefCloud**, so growth form alone is never enough: the app must teach genus recognition.
- Path in the app = **Learn → Practice → Identify** (the order RAs work in), plus **My progress** and a Pierre-only **admin** back-office (`admin.html`).

## Standing rules from Pierre (do not break these)

These came from Pierre's explicit feedback. When in doubt, follow them over your own taste, and ask him rather than guessing.

### Content and sources
1. **Never contradict or "correct" the IMR course or its lecturers** in the app. Keep the IMR wording (families, lecture lines, mnemonics, spellings). Where another source differs, add at most a discreet, dated, sourced note ("Source X (year) says…", shown as "Other sources" from `gen[g].info`), never "the lecture is wrong/outdated". Obvious slide slips are silently not reproduced and mentioned to Pierre only. When a source is old, trust IMR unless a very reliable *recent* source says otherwise; IMR trainers may be more up to date than the literature. Why: Pierre is new at IMR and the app is used there; visibly correcting the trainers would hurt his relationships ("Je ne veux pas me faire des ennemis").
2. **Never invent categories or information.** Groupings come only from existing references: IMR Lecture 6 groups (1-11), Coral Finder 2022 categories/boxes, the 7 IMR growth forms, or a mix. Every clue, label or pair carries its source (slide number, CF page/box). Claude-made look-alike "families" were rejected.
3. **IMR lecture cues are the most important content.** Two levels: the **ID tip** (`gen[g].qt`, from `tools/id_tips.txt`) is SHORT, 2-3 essentials, shown everywhere (quiz feedback, cards, ID tips view, comparisons) in a highlighted box; the full lecture cues (`gen[g].imr`, incl. review slides and presenter notes) appear only on the genus sheet under "IMR lecture notes".
4. **Growth form is an entry point only**, never genus-specific and never a strict filter (rank, don't filter). Photos use the 7 IMR (AIMS lifeform) forms: Branching, Tabulate, Encrusting, Foliose/Plating, Massive, Submassive, Mushroom (+ "x" too close to tell, + separate "polyps extended" flag). One form per photo, the dominant visible one. Never group or pick genera by growth form either (Pierre, 2026-11-03, on the custom quiz's selection screen: many genera take 3-4 forms or more, only the solitary mushroom corals keep one): a list of genera is A-Z, the slate order or the IMR lecture groups. The polyps flag ✿ only for polyps visibly extended at colony scale, only for the Coral Finder "extended by day" genera (Alveopora, Duncanopsammia, Goniopora, Heliofungia, Plerogyra, Tubastraea), never on macro photos.
5. **Everything produced is in English** (UI, docs, data, commit messages, artifact titles). Pierre chats in French; only chat may be French.
6. Open discrepancies between sources are listed in `docs/ROADMAP.md` ("Questions for an IMR trainer"). Don't settle them yourself. The slide text is not the whole course: some points are only said in class or only shown in an uncaptioned photo, so a criterion missing from the slides is "check with an IMR trainer", never "not taught".
6a. **Fish names and presence** (Pierre, 2026-11-03): the name shown and expected is the EventMeasure / IMR reference PDF name (EventMeasure's fixed pick list may lag WoRMS); the WoRMS accepted name is an indication beside it, and both are accepted when typed. A likely slip in the PDF is kept and flagged for Pierre, never corrected. Pierre's own iNaturalist observations (`ppericard`) identified to the species count as confirmed even when Needs ID (his only); a species seen only as Needs ID near Dauin is "not confirmed near Dauin", never "never seen". ARCHITECTURE.md "Fish content".
6b. **Starred genera** (Duncanopsammia, Alveopora, Merulina, Cyphastrea, Halomitra) are on the slate *because* they look like surveyed genera: never show them as a separate "not covered" group; `shownGroup()` places them in the lecture group of their Coral Finder look-alikes (display only). On the one-at-a-time path a starred genus opens only after the surveyed genera its card compares it to have been met (Pierre, 2026-11-03: a beginner met *Duncanopsammia*, "Like *Turbinaria*…", before *Turbinaria*); the references come from the data (`core.js` `starRefs`), never a list in code; Open all still opens everything at once.

### Photos
7. **Photo priority in every context: IMR lecture photos first, then Pierre's own (iNat user `ppericard`), then other iNaturalist observers and Coral Finder book photos only to fill gaps.** Covers and the first 3 ("featured") photos per view: IMR before Pierre's. IMR photos are never auto-rated Poor and never auto-excluded. Exception: the Coral Finder mixes sources on purpose for variety.
8. **Pierre's curation choices (`curation.json`) are never changed automatically**: no script, rebuild or import may overwrite them. Only step-by-step Undo of his own actions; **no bulk reset/wipe button of any kind** (he refused it twice). Never overwrite `curation.json` in a PR: Pierre publishes it straight to `main` from the admin; rebase and keep `main`'s copy. The admin's Publish never writes a whole copy: it re-reads the published file and merges the draft onto it key by key (`merge3()`, PR #240, after a whole-copy publish from a stale tab lost curation on 2026-11-01). Keep it that way if you touch `save()` in `admin.js`. The **auto-sort** (Pierre, 2026-11-03) is the one thing that writes choices for him, and only through that same path: at each admin load it puts the candidate pass's sure judgments into his draft, for photos he has not decided, never replacing a value he gave, each one logged as auto (`curation.json` `auto`) and undoable one by one; his Publish sends them. Never make it write `curation.json` any other way.
9. Three views per genus: **colony**, **close-up** (10-30 cm part of a colony; code name `medium`, labelled "Closer" before 2026-11-01) and **corallites** (macro). A genus may have 2 levels: colony + close-up (its macro photos, also labelled "Close-up", Pierre 2026-11-01), never duplicate a photo to fill a view. Cover = mid-distance view, never macro.
10. Never use photos showing several corals in games (`p.multi`; Pierre can clear a wrong mark with curation `single`). **Coral Finder book photos** (© R. Kelley / BYOGUIDES, `gen[g].book`) are **never in games**; on a genus sheet they appear only when Pierre pins one, or to fill a gap after every field photo; the Coral Finder adds up to 3 approved ones for variety. Pierre can switch them off site-wide (admin, curation `book: false`). **IMR lecture photos are game photos** (since PR #227) and count in admin Coverage › Games. "Poor" means identifiable but low visual quality (ReefCloud-like): Poor photos are used at the hard end of games; unidentifiable photos are excluded, not rated Poor.
11. Provenance tags never on thumbnails; credit in the lightbox. Games: once the answer is out, every photo shows a small clickable source pill (`photoSrc()` in `app.js`: observer + iNat link, or IMR lecture / Coral Finder label), never before the answer (it would give the genus away); hidden on phones, where the enlarged view carries the clickable source (Pierre, 2026-11-01).

11b. **Players can report a problem from every game** once the answer is out (wrong answer + the right genus, several corals, bad photo, other, note), and, discreetly, from everywhere it makes sense: any enlarged photo, the bottom of a genus sheet, "Send feedback" at the end of the ? help of every page and on About and credits (Pierre, 2026-11-01; the page footer went on 2026-11-03, see DESIGN.md "Footer and credits"). New pages get feedback through the ? help automatically; use `repBtn()` for anything more specific. Pierre sees who sent each report in admin To do (views All and Reports, the former Triage), because he trusts some players more than others (2026-11-01). Reports never change anything by themselves.

### Learning logic
12. **Mastery never depends on time** (no spaced-repetition days, no decay, no "refresh"). It is a practical tool, not a retention or daily-habit app (no streaks, lessons, Duolingo-style units). Someone should be able to master everything in a few hours. **One exception, time to plan the comeback after a break** (Pierre, 2026-11-02: "the no-time rule was there because the goal is not to bring people back every day like Duolingo; the goal is to learn as fast as possible, in a few hours or days. But for the comeback, time must be taken into account"): the time since the last answers decides the questions after a break (a new visit after 30 minutes without an answer opens no genus in its first 10 answers; the FSRS forgetting curve plans a comeback on genera answered right; `model.js` "Comeback after a break", docs/LEARNING.md). Progress (`s.pg`), mastery (`s.mx`) and the Elo ability (`s.th`) never decay with time: only answers move them.
13. **A wrong answer counts against both genera**: the correct one and the one wrongly given.
14. Difficulty is adaptive and continuous: per-genus player ability vs photo difficulty on one Elo scale (Pelánek 2016), ~80% target success, guaranteed variety and new photos, more single-view questions as the level rises (details in ARCHITECTURE.md). No manual Easy/Medium/Hard selector. Pierre's own answers bias photo difficulty (he knows the photos): admin Stats has a "Without me" switch; the games still use everyone's answers until RAs have played (ROADMAP). Starred genera are always included in Practice. Never split practice by lecture group.
14a. **Millepora and the bonus genera** (Pierre, 2026-11-01, changed 2026-11-02): *Millepora* is a slate genus for everything in the games and the progress (`BENTHIC`), with a warning on its first card and its sheet that it is not a hard coral nor on the IMR coral list, but on the IMR benthic slate. The other genera off the slate that are common at Dauin (`BONUS`: Isopora, Leptoseris, Heliopora, Sandalolitha, Pleuractis) are **opt-in**: offered once by a card when the player has met every slate genus (Add them / Not now; never in the first 10 answers of a visit or during a comeback, rule 12), and ⚙ Bonus genera On / Off any time (`Q.bon`). Once on they open one at a time like slate genera (no threshold, no weight cap of their own). They never count in the progress, their ID tips are the Coral Finder's (labelled so), their game photos (Millepora's too) are Research Grade iNat (or Pierre's) only. Details: ARCHITECTURE.md "Bonus genera". **No genus-specific case in code** (Pierre, 2026-11-03): the game genera (slate + Millepora + bonus) and the Finder-only ones (the other `data.json` `x` genera) are defined once, `core.js` `BENTHIC`, `BONUS`, `slateOf()`, `gameOf()` (Python: `tools/genera.py`); use them, never `n === 'Millepora'` or a `!DATA.gen[n].x` filter for "game genus". In the admin the game genera come first (Candidates, Game coverage); Finder-only genera get no Corallites candidates and a cap of `CAP.sheet` (6) colonies per view.
14b. **All photos of one game question come from ONE observation (same colony)** (Pierre, 2026-11-01). Never top up a missing zoom level with another colony: a colony with one view becomes a single-view question.
14c. **Answer choices get harder gradually** (Pierre, 2026-11-01: "at the very start it must be simple", traps appear little by little). In the photo quiz the wrong choices for a genus start easy (genera sharing no IMR growth form with it), and its look-alikes come in one by one, and soon (one after the first right answer, all three after about 5: look-alikes are one of the ways genera are learned), as the player's Elo ability on that genus rises (`FORMAT`, `trapsFor()`, `distractors()` in `app.js`). Duels stay look-alike pairs by design. Official IMR / Coral Finder look-alikes only *seed* the confusions: real player answers then decide which genera get confused (Pierre, 2026-11-01; confusion scores), pooled over every player first, then each player's own mix-ups weigh in as their answers on the pair build up (personal path, `pairConf`; performance only, never time). A genus never answered starts from the player's global level (`playerStart`, `core.js`: an Elo over all their answers, shrunk while they are few), so a strong player gets traps and harder photos on a new genus from its first question; "Learned" is still earned genus by genus.
14d. **Achievements reward learning, never time** (Pierre, 2026-11-01): unlocked by learning milestones (quarters of the way to learned, genera learned, progress, runs of right answers, duels, corallites, hard photos, Elo), never by time spent or days played; spread out so the first come within a few dozen questions (`core.js` `ACH`; check a new one's timing with the real game before adding it).
14e. **Learned = the silent mastery exam** (Pierre, 2026-11-01): a genus fills up to 95% with its progress, a moving average of its answers (since 2026-11-02, Pierre: right answers against its look-alikes count most, each point harder to earn than the last; docs/LEARNING.md); learned (100%) takes 5 exam questions slipped unannounced into the photo quiz, several answers apart, each as hard as the game gets (one photo alone, never seen, look-alike traps), all right, at least 2 of them typed from memory (Pierre, 2026-11-03: typed questions come in progressively as the level on a genus rises, never as an on/off mode; `model.js` `TYPED`). Any miss on the genus, or giving it as the answer for another one, restarts it. No milestone that a player can pass by luck or in a few questions.
14f. **The simplest algorithm that works** (Pierre, 2026-11-02: "the simpler the algorithm, the better; with 50,000 parts and 50,000 variables it gets ever harder to calibrate"). Don't add a variable, a special case or a hard-coded ramp to patch a problem: first rethink the rule or recalibrate the existing settings (example: the opening of genera became a slope on `OPEN.K` instead of new duel/opening knobs, docs/LEARNING.md). Any change to the learning path is checked over several seeds (`tests/profiles.smoke.js` profiles), not one run, and its effect on what players really learn with `node tools/learnsim.js` (5 seeds per profile, before and after, the same seeds; docs/LEARNING.md "Learning-outcome simulation"). Five is enough (Pierre, 2026-11-03: "4 or 5 players were plenty to get an idea", no p-values wanted); add seeds only when two versions differ by less than their spread on the measure that decides.
14g. **Check algorithm changes against the real players, not only the simulation (Pierre, 2026-11-03).** Any change to the learning algorithm (Elo, progress, draw, opening, difficulty, typed questions…) is checked twice before its PR: in `tools/learnsim.js` (5 seeds, the four profiles: no learning path may break) and on the real answer log (read-only replay of the sheet's Answers tab, or a saved extract kept outside the repo): the change must not move the app's estimates away from what players really do, and should bring them closer (next-answer log-loss and calibration, Expected against actual, overall and by level band and question format). Where a replay cannot judge a change (a different draw asks other questions), say what was measured instead. The PR shows both tables; LEARNING.md keeps them.
14g. **Compute once, not on every phone** (Pierre, 2026-11-02): what can be computed once (in the sync sheet's stats, or in `data.json` at build time) and served to the app is preferred over recomputing it on each phone or in each admin session.
15. Visual recognition and fast side-by-side comparison beat theory. The 5-question observation routine is only for the very first contact. In the field people recognise genera by overall look; on photos they use the Coral Finder: growth form first, then a quick visual comparison of the candidates. Quiz feedback shows corallite traits and lecture cues, never growth form as the reason.
15b. Short-term goal: ID on ideal photos, or on dives with time to look closely. ReefCloud point drills, realistic/degraded images, RealityScan and Metashape material are medium term (ROADMAP "Parked").

### UI and layout
16. **Declutter**: only essentials on a page. No page titles or intro paragraphs; explanations go behind the shared "?" help button (`pageHelp()` moves any top-level `h2`, `.lede`, `.tips`, `.gloss`, `h3.label` there automatically). One shared mechanism for everything; no per-page exceptions; code homogeneous.
17. **Every game/exercise fits ONE screen in every state** (including feedback after a wrong answer); controls and answers in a side column on wide screens; games may bleed up to 1680 px (`.bleed`).
18. Fixed page width 1280 px; the header never changes size. Light theme default (⚙ Theme). One colour palette site-wide, chosen in ⚙ Colours: Lagoon, Ocean, Seagrass, Twilight, **Depth (default, Pierre's pick)**; ⚙ Colour-blind On/Off swaps green/red for blue/orange (right/wrong, review). Any new colour must come from the palette tokens (`:root[data-palette]`, `[data-cvd]` in `app.css`), in light and dark. Strong colour belongs to the learning-level scale; IMR group colours are muted tints with dark text (number + title carry the group), so one hue never means two things on a screen (design review, 2026-11-01). Genus names are true italics: the self-hosted fonts (`fonts/`, `@font-face` at the top of `app.css`, since 2026-11-01) include Source Sans 3 italic (variable 400-700) and Barlow Condensed italic 600/700; never rely on faux italics. Readable text is at least 12 px. **Design tokens and components are in [`docs/DESIGN.md`](docs/DESIGN.md)**: font sizes only from the `--fs-*` scale, radii `--r-xs/--r-sm/--r/--pill`, shadows `--shadow/--shadow-hover`, buttons `.next` / `.next.sec` / `.linkish` / `.theme`, one segmented look for every exclusive choice, one SVG icon set. Photo sizes S/M/L (default M), remembered per page (`SIZE_PAGES`, `pageKey()`).
19. **Responsive**: one code base, mobile-first CSS, exactly 3 size classes: phone < 600 px, tablet 600-1023 px (also a half-screen desktop window), desktop ≥ 1024 px. Same values in CSS and `app.js` `MQ` (`isPhone()`, `isStacked()`). Touch vs mouse by `hover`/`pointer` media, never width. No other breakpoints. Test at 360, 412, 768, 960, 1280, 1440, 1920 px wide.
19b. **No third-party scripts** (the origin `ppericard.github.io` is shared and the admin token lives on it). A new external image or API host must be added to the CSP meta in `index.html`/`admin.html` (see ARCHITECTURE.md "Security model").
20. No horizontal sliders. Visual and dynamic beats text. New features must not degrade the look.
20b. Settings that were removed on purpose (don't bring them back): the "Photos from Dauin / Philippines / All" filter (PR #230; the IMR/own-first rule already does that job), the Reef theme, the quiz level selector.
20c. **Never hardcode the genus count** ("39", "40") in user-facing text: it changes (Millepora joined in 2026-11-02; bonus genera): use `slateGenera().length`. Docs may say 39 for the IMR coral slate, 40 with Millepora. A one-time announcement of the change itself (`RECALC`) may name the number.
20d. **Sign-in never on My progress**: signed out = a "Sign in" button in the header on every page; signed in = account line and Sign out at the top of ⚙. The welcome tour bubble never covers what it points at. An existing account is entered only after "Is it you?" (its answers and last play), on every device; simple codes stay allowed (Pierre, 2026-11-03: players type only their first name, so names collide).
21. Home = two big doors only ("Learn & practise", "Identify a coral"), plus one credit line under them, all in one screen; Learn & practise is the primary door, Identify the secondary one (Pierre, 2026-11-03; DESIGN.md "Footer and credits", "Home hierarchy"). Phones: bottom tab bar, no Tools/Lab tab (hammer icon in header).
22. **Lab/Tools prototypes graduate into the main app only with Pierre's explicit approval.** Propose; never promote yourself.
23. **Admin is for Pierre only**: minimal, no explanatory text on the page (tooltips = bare names; help behind the admin "?"), one-click segmented controls (reuse the existing segmented style, no default browser buttons, no lone ✓ marks), header on one line. Everyday per-photo actions (× exclude, Reject, Remove) are one click with **no confirmation**: Undo is the safety net (Pierre, PR #265); confirmations only for bulk or site-wide actions (Publish, Validate N, CF book switch). Same information = a filter; different information = a separate view (Pierre, PR #242). The admin has **nine tabs in three header groups** (Pierre, 2026-11-03, after "regroup the admin menus" of 2026-11-01): To do (To do, Coverage), Photos (Genera, Candidates, Photo review, Look-alikes, Photo stats), Players (Players, Activity); the tabs of a group are in a row under the header, the views of a tab in a segmented switch at its right. A new admin page becomes a **view of an existing tab** (`GROUPS` in `admin.js`), not a new tab; an old address must keep landing on its tab and view (`ALIAS`).

## How to make a change

1. Branch from `main` (fetch first: `main` moves often, see below). Run locally: `python3 -m http.server 8000` and open <http://localhost:8000>. To see and try the admin without a token, open <http://localhost:8000/admin.html?demo>: every page works on made-up players, answers and reports (`admin-demo.js` answers the sync script and GitHub calls in memory; nothing is sent, Publish is kept in memory, its storage keys are prefixed `demo:`). Use it for screenshots and to test an admin change; when you change a sync reply or an admin call, keep `admin-demo.js` in step. There is no build step. Tests: `node --test tests/*.test.js` (Node only, no npm), plus the page smoke test `node tests/pages.smoke.js` the question draw test `node tests/draw.smoke.js` and the admin demo test `node tests/admin.smoke.js` (all need Playwright; CI installs it in a temporary folder, never add a package.json); add one when you change logic they cover. Pure logic shared by app and admin (curation merge, progress merge, photo Elo) lives in `core.js` so it can be tested. Every PR runs `.github/workflows/checks.yml` (JS syntax, tests, JSON, `data.json` layout, Python, versions, and the page smoke test in its own job `pages`).
2. **Formatting** (`.git-blame-ignore-revs` lists the reformatting commits; run `git config blame.ignoreRevsFile .git-blame-ignore-revs` once for local blame): JavaScript and CSS (app, admin, Lab, tests; not `tools/`, HTML or JSON) are formatted with Prettier 3.9.10 and `.prettierrc.json` (print width 140). Before pushing: `npx --yes prettier@3.9.10 --write "*.js" "*.css" "proto/*.js" "proto/*.css" "tests/*.js"` (no package.json); the PR check fails otherwise. Prettier 3.3.3 (an old version picked from memory) dropped array elements inside a template literal in `proto/anatomy.js`, found by an AST comparison (2026-11-01): after a formatter upgrade, compare the syntax trees before and after, and run it until the files stop changing.
3. **Cache busting (easy to forget):** when you change a shell file, bump its `?v=N` in `index.html` (and in `app.js` for `data.json`/`forms.json` fetches), the same entry in `sw.js` `SHELL`, and the `sw.js` cache name `V`. For a Lab file, bump `LAB_V` in `proto/lab.js` and `proto/lab.*?v=` in `index.html` and `sw.js`. Other parallel branches bump these too: on rebase, take a number above `main`'s. **`python3 tools/check_versions.py --base origin/main --fix` does all of this for you** (run it after rebasing, before pushing); the PR check fails when a changed file kept its old number.
4. Editing `data.json` by hand is normal (the original build script reads a scratchpad that no longer exists). **Write it only with `tools/datajson.py`** (candidates waiting live in `cand.json`, admin only: read both with `load_all()`, write with `dump_all()`, which keeps in `data.json` only the candidates `curation.json` names) (`from datajson import dump` then `dump(d, path)`, never `json.dump`): one top-level key per line and one genus per line in `gen`, `course`, `cand`, `x` and `x.gen`, compact within a line, so two edits to different genera merge cleanly. After a hand edit, `python3 tools/datajson.py` puts the file back in that layout; the PR check (`--check`) fails otherwise. Prefer a small, re-runnable `tools/apply_*.py` script that documents the change.
5. Check visually with Playwright (Chromium) at the widths in rule 19, light and dark, phone and desktop; check that games fit one screen. Open the browser console: no errors. **After a big change to the interface or the flow, replay the whole app as a brand-new player** (Pierre, 2026-11-02, after an RA found the tour and the first genus card on screen together: the tests pre-set "tour seen" or "cards seen", so no test met both; not needed for internal code or small fixes, Pierre the same day): an empty browser, phone and desktop, light and dark, from the first load through the tour, the first cards, a few dozen questions (Again, duels, the offer, achievements), My progress, the other games, Identify, Tools, help and settings; screenshots at each step, every overlay alone on screen and closable.
6. Open a PR with a short English description (what changed, why, screenshots for UI). Merge it yourself once checked, as a **squash merge** whose title ends with ` (#N)` (that is how `git log` reads). Never force-push `main`; never rewrite history without Pierre's explicit go-ahead. A history rewrite to shrink `.git` is planned, not declined (Pierre, 2026-11-02): first move the photos to Cloudflare R2, then remove `img/` from the repo, then rewrite history so the old photos leave `.git`; ask Pierre for the go before the force-push and keep a backup clone (ROADMAP "Photos out of the repo").
   - Two kinds of commits land on `main` without a PR: Pierre's admin **Publish** ("Photo curation: …", writes `curation.json` only) and the GitHub Action that follows it ("Candidate photos: …", writes `data.json` and `img/`). So rebase on `origin/main` right before merging. With one genus per line, edits to different genera merge by themselves; on a real `data.json` conflict, take `main`'s copy and re-run your `tools/apply_*.py` script rather than hand-merging.
   - A merge to `main` is live on GitHub Pages within a minute or two; open tabs pick up the new shell on the next visit (service worker) and a new `curation.json` within 30 min or when the tab comes back into view.
7. If you changed `tools/sync/Code.gs`: bump `VERSION` (header comment and constant). From v11 the script **updates itself** from `main` within about 15 minutes of the merge (`autoUpdate`, Pierre, 2026-11-01: no more copy and paste), (set up by Pierre on 2026-11-01: standard Cloud project "IMR Coral ID sync", consent screen published, "Self-update ready"); check the live `v` about 15 minutes after merging, and the sheet's "Deploy log" tab says what happened. Never remove `autoUpdate` (the updater refuses such a version, so the script would stay behind) and keep `tools/sync/appsscript.json` in step (a new Google service = a new scope there = Pierre must accept it once: tell him). Deployed: **v11** verified live 2026-11-01; v12 (anonymous answers, flood caps) deploys itself after its merge. Security model and abuse handling: [`docs/SECURITY.md`](docs/SECURITY.md). The app keeps working with an older script, so a change must stay backward compatible. `Code.gs` embeds `core.js` between `// BEGIN core.js` and `// END core.js` (reached as `CORE_`; the sheet replays each player's progress with it, v28): after any change to `core.js`, run `python3 tools/sync_core.py --fix` and bump `VERSION` (CI runs `--check`), so the live script always runs the app's engine. If you change the Elo in `stats_`, change its copy `eloOf()` in `core.js` too (with the photo memory estimate, `MEM`, from v26), and likewise the pair counts `conf` in `stats_` and `confusionOf()` (`tests/core.test.js` checks both agree).
7a. **The app never gets ahead of the sheet script (Pierre, 2026-11-03).** After a merge the app is live within minutes, the script only at its next self-update (up to 15 minutes later). So any app feature that needs a sheet script version N (a new call, a new reply field, a new rule the app relies on) turns on only when the script reports `v` >= N in its replies (load, stats), and does exactly what it did before otherwise, silently: no error, no half-working screen, no lost answer. A change the old script handles as it is (it stores the new value as it comes, and every number derived from it is recomputed from the answer log, as typed answers `|t` with v27) needs no gate: say so in the PR. The PR states which script version the app waits for. After merging a `Code.gs` change, read the sheet's "Deploy log" tab (read-only) until the new version is deployed, and tell Pierre.
8. If you discussed an idea and did not build it: add it to `docs/ROADMAP.md`.
9. **Keep the docs current in the same PR (Pierre, 2026-11-01).** A change is not done until the docs say what the code now does. Before merging, check and update as needed: this file (a new lasting rule from Pierre, a changed workflow), `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (a new page, hash, setting, data key, `curation.json` key, admin mode, sync endpoint or Code.gs version, model constant), `docs/ROADMAP.md` (ship = move to "Done recently" with the PR number; deferred = add a line; new "don't do X" = Declined) and `README.md` (anything a newcomer sees). Say "Docs: none needed" in the PR description when nothing applies. The goal: another agent (Codex, a Claude Project, a new developer) can take over from the repo alone, with no chat history or memory.
10. Every ~20 merged PRs, or when Pierre asks, do a full documentation pass: re-check each doc against the code (not against memory) and fix what drifted.
11. **Remind Pierre when a genus runs short of candidates (Pierre, 2026-11-02).** The candidate search (`tools/fetch_local.py`) runs on request only. At the start of a working session and in recaps, run `python3 tools/cand_gaps.py` (game genera with fewer than 5 candidate photos waiting in `cand.json`, listed only when iNaturalist still has something the search would take near Dauin or in the Philippines; Pierre: the search stays in the Philippines, nearest first: within 250 km of Dauin, then the rest of the country; and only when the whole country has nothing left, photos taken elsewhere of species confirmed in the Philippines (Research Grade there); never a species not seen in the Philippines (2026-11-03), and no reminder when it cannot find anything better); if it lists any, tell Pierre and offer to run the search for them. The choice of photos stays his (rule 27c).
12. **Announce a recalculation of everyone's progress with a real pop-up (Pierre, 2026-11-02).** When a change recalculates every player's progress, returning players see one pop-up at their next visit, in plain words, without the algorithm's details (`RECALC` in `progress.js`: give it a new `ID` and text). Discreet notes are for new features, not for this. One exception so far: the photo memory recalculation of 2026-11-03 is silent (Pierre: the bars only move down 1-3 points; `core.js` `memRecalc`). A learned mark taken away by a recalculation is never silent (Pierre, 2026-11-03: players know they test an early beta): run the recalculation through `model.js` `mxRecalc`, which tells the players concerned once with a card and takes back the learned-based achievements they no longer meet (docs/LEARNING.md "Learned under the former model"). **Announcement cards are never held** (Pierre, 2026-11-03): a card that announces a rule change, a learned mark taken away or a big new app version shows as soon as the player opens or reconnects to the app (once the first page is drawn and the progress is in, and right after a pull that brings the change), on whatever page they land on, once per device, marked seen by its button; no visit floor (`noOpen`) and no prompt spacing (`promptOk`). It only waits for another card or the tour already on screen, then follows it (`progress.js` `recalcNotice`, `mxLostNotice`, `rvNotice`). The other prompts (sign-in nudge, one-tap question, open-all and bonus genera offers) keep `promptOk`.
13. **Your tests never reach the real sheet (Pierre, 2026-11-03).** The app sends nothing to the sheet from a local copy or a browser driven by a program (`app.js` `LOCAL`: localhost, 127.x, `file:`, `navigator.webdriver`), signed in or not: signing in, saving and loading an account go through `app.js` `sheetFetch`, which a local copy answers only from a test's `window.SHEET_STUB` (`tests/pages.smoke.js`). Never work around it (a made-up host name, a patched `LOCAL`, a stubbed `navigator.webdriver`): network mocks (`route`) miss the requests a page sends while it unloads, and an agent's test that served the app under a made-up host name put 100 made-up answers and 6 events in the sheet. To test the sending code, run it against stubs (`tests/sync.test.js`, a wrapped `fetch` as in `tests/net.smoke.js` check 11) and map the sync script's hosts to this computer (`--host-resolver-rules=MAP script.google.com 127.0.0.1, MAP script.googleusercontent.com 127.0.0.1`) so nothing that escapes can reach Google. If test data did reach the sheet, tell Pierre at once: which rows, and how to remove them.
14. **Every change to a progress rule appends a rules version (Pierre, 2026-11-03: progress is recalculated from the whole answer log, automatically, never by a human click, now and whenever the progress engine changes).** A change to anything that moves a player's progress (what an answer weighs, the moving average, the ability step, the photo memory, what the replay keeps from the device: `core.js` `replayProgress`, `replayedOf` and what they call) adds an entry at the end of `core.js` `RVS` (`v` the next number, `at`, `why`, `msg`). The sync sheet then replays every player's log again with the new rules at their next sign-in, load or save, and each device adopts it once (`Code.gs` `replay_`, `sync.js` `replayAdopt`; docs/LEARNING.md "Full replay"). Run `python3 tools/sync_core.py --fix` (core.js is embedded in `Code.gs`; CI checks it) and bump `Code.gs` `VERSION`. **Before merging, ask Pierre whether that version gets a `msg`** (an announcement card, `{t, p}` in plain words, shown once at app open on every device that skipped it, never held; rule 12) **and its text**; the default is none (`msg: null`); `only: 'changed'` limits it to the players whose progress % the replay moved, `{from}` and `{to}` in its text giving the two %.
