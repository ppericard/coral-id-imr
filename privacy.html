Privacy

IMR Reef ID, a training tool for coral identification (and reef fish, in beta) at the Institute for Marine Research (IMR), Dauin, Philippines. Run by Pierre Pericard. Last updated 8 November 2026 (the technical reports: what they hold, the visit report sent once a visit, and how their device id is linked to your answers; each answer now carries the device id).

Without signing in

Your progress and settings stay in your browser, on your device (local storage). Your answers in the games (the genus or fish shown, your answer, the choices offered, the photos shown, the game, the time and your device's time zone offset, how many seconds you took, whether you enlarged a photo and, on a question where you type the name, what you typed) are also sent, without any name or code, under a random id made on your device, to the Google Sheets spreadsheet described below. Once there are enough of them to show real play (not random clicks), they help rate how hard each photo is and which genera get mixed up, as everyone's do. If you sign in later, they are attached to your name.

The app also sends a few short technical reports, signed in or not: see Technical reports below.

If you sign in

You sign in with a name of your choice and a 4-digit code. The following is then saved in a Google Sheets spreadsheet owned by Pierre Pericard, through a Google Apps Script:

  • your name, and your code in a scrambled form (a hash): the code itself is not stored;
  • your progress on each genus, and your settings in the app;
  • each answer you give in the games: what was shown (a coral genus, or a fish family or genus, with the fish species in the photo) and the level it was asked at, your answer, the choices offered, the photos shown, the game, the time and your device's time zone offset (to tell morning from evening where you are), how many seconds you took and whether you enlarged a photo; on a question where you type the name, what you typed and whether you retyped a misspelt name right;
  • your progress (%) and whether you want to be on the leaderboard.

This is used to save your progress across devices, to let IMR trainers follow the training, and to rate how hard each photo is. The photo ratings and the game statistics use anonymous ids, not names; apart from the leaderboard below, only the app's administrators see names.

Which pages you open

The app itself counts which of its pages you open during a visit (for example the quiz, the genus sheets, Genera, My progress), how many times and for how long each one is on screen, which genus sheets you open, how you reached each one (from a quiz answer, the Learn pages, My progress or elsewhere), and how many questions you had answered by then. When you leave or switch away from the app, this short summary is sent to the same spreadsheet, filed with your answers: under the same random id as your answers if you are not signed in; if you are, your name and code go with it so they can be checked, and only the anonymous id of your account is kept with the summary, never your name. The device id of the technical reports (below) is kept with it too. It contains no text you typed, no photos and no location. It is used only to see how the app is used (for example whether the genus sheets help) and to improve the course. No tracking script or outside service is involved, and nothing is shared.

Technical reports

Your device also makes a second random id of its own, the device id. The short reports below are sent under it to the same spreadsheet, signed in or not, never with your name. Each one carries the page of the app you were on, the app version and the type of browser (for example "Chrome 130 mobile").

  • Once a visit, how the app loaded: the first page you opened and the next one, whether and when you opened the quiz, how many seconds the first page and the first quiz photo took to appear, the speed of your connection as the app measured it, how much was downloaded, whether the app came from the copy kept on your device, whether the photo Saver was on, the theme (light or dark), the width of the window, whether the app was opened from its icon on your home screen or in a browser tab, and the name of the GitHub server that delivered the app (it tells roughly which region it came from). This helps make the app load faster on slow connections and shows how it is used.
  • When something goes wrong: an error (its message and where it happened in the app's code), a photo that does not load, or a save that fails.
  • An app that did not update: if your device keeps running an older version of the app, the spreadsheet notes it (the version, and how long the page had stayed open), at most once every few hours.
  • The one-tap question and the tips: now and then, after an answer, the app asks whether the questions are too easy, about right or too hard. It reports that the question was shown and, if you tap an answer, your answer, with the game, how many questions you had answered in this game session, how many of your last 10 were right, your level and how many of the course's corals or fish you have met. You can ignore the question (Not now). The "Did you know?" tips are reported the same way: shown, and which button you tapped.

How the device id is linked to you. On its own, the device id says nothing about who you are. But each summary of the pages you open is filed with both the device id and the anonymous id of your answers (or of your account, if you are signed in), as said above. Each answer you give is also stored with the device id of the device you gave it on, signed in or not, so the app's administrators can see which device each answer came from. So the app's administrators can see which answers were given on a device, and which technical reports came from it, for example to fix a problem for the person who met it, or to see on how many devices someone plays. Your name is never stored with these reports. If you delete your account, your answers and page summaries go with it, so its device ids are no longer linked to you. If an administrator deletes an account but keeps its answers anonymously (they then only help rate the photos), the device id is removed from those answers.

Leaderboard

If you sign in, your name, your progress (%) and your Elo score (your level, computed from your answers) appear on the public leaderboard of the app (🏆), one per subject (corals, fish), where anyone using the app can see them. You can leave it at any time: ⚙ Settings, Leaderboard: Off. Nothing else about you is shown there.

Problem reports and feedback

When you report a problem or send feedback, the report, your note and your name (if you give it or are signed in) are saved in the same spreadsheet, so the photos and texts can be fixed.

What is not collected

No advertising, no third-party analytics or tracking scripts, no cookies, no e-mail address, no location (the app never asks your device where you are). Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Other services the pages load from

Like any website, these see your device's IP address when they serve a file: GitHub Pages (the app itself and its fonts), Cloudflare (the coral photos, from its R2 storage), iNaturalist (some coral photos, the fish photos, and any photo whose copy on Cloudflare cannot be reached), FishBase (a few fish photos) and Google (the sync script, when you are signed in or send a report).

Deleting your data

Sign out in ⚙ to clear the app from your device. To delete your account, do it yourself in the app: About and credits, Delete my account (signed in, with your code; the app asks twice). You can also ask Pierre Pericard at IMR, or open an issue at github.com/ppericard/coral-id-imr.

Either way, everything is deleted: your name, your saved progress and settings, every answer you gave, your problem reports and feedback, and the summaries of the pages you opened. Your name also disappears from the app's technical logs, and from the statistics reports, which are computed again without your account. Only your name and your scrambled code are kept, in a list of removed accounts, so that a device still signed in with them is told the account was deleted instead of creating it again; nothing else about you is kept. When you delete it yourself, the device you used is signed out and cleared too.

You may instead ask Pierre to keep your answers anonymously: the same is deleted, except that your answers and your reports are kept without your name or anything that leads back to you. The answers are then used only to rate how hard each photo is and which genera get mixed up, never as a player; the reports only to fix the photos and texts they are about.
