Privacy

IMR Reef ID, a training tool for identifying corals, reef fish and sea slugs (in beta), at the Institute for Marine Research (IMR), Dauin, Philippines. Run by Pierre Pericard. Last updated 19 November 2026.

What is kept

  • If you sign in: your name, your 4-digit code in a scrambled form (the code itself is not stored), your settings, your progress and the answers you give in the games, live games included.
  • If you do not sign in: your answers and progress, under a random id made on your device, with no name.
  • Short usage and technical reports (which pages you open, how the app loaded, errors), never with your name.
  • The problem reports and feedback you send.

No advertising, no tracking scripts, no cookies, no e-mail address, no location.

Why

To save your progress across devices, to let IMR's trainers follow the training, to rate how hard each photo is, and to fix and improve the app.

Who sees it

Pierre Pericard and the app's administrators at IMR. If you sign in, your name, progress and level appear on the app's leaderboards; you can leave them in ⚙ Settings, Leaderboard: Off. In a live game, everyone in the room sees the name you type there and the top scores. Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Where and how long

In a Google Sheets spreadsheet owned by Pierre Pericard, for as long as your account exists, with a weekly backup copy in his private cloud storage. The app is served by GitHub Pages and its photos by Cloudflare, iNaturalist and FishBase, which only serve files. Your progress is also kept in your browser.

Deleting your data

Delete your account yourself in the app: About and credits, Delete my account. Your name, progress, answers, reports and leaderboard places are deleted. What remains: your name in a list of removed accounts (so a device still signed in is told), the audit log's notes about your account under an anonymous id, and the backup copies made before the deletion. Signing out in ⚙ clears the app from your device.

Contact

To see, correct or delete your data, or for any question: Pierre Pericard at IMR, or an issue at github.com/ppericard/coral-id-imr.
