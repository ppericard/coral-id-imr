## Check 2C: is the app's per-unit ability centred? (Q136)

`checks.js`, the rule declared in #1310: "clearly negative" (not centred) = the Spearman correlation of each unit's mean
δ over players with the live unit offset below 0 at permutation p < 0.05, and below 0 in both halves of the players'
order of meeting units.

| | Units | Spearman with live gd | p | First half | Second half | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corals (primary) | 39 | −0.77 | < 0.001 | −0.64 (30 units, p < 0.001) | −0.50 (12 units, p 0.10) | **not centred** |
| Fish (exploratory) | 29 | −0.91 | < 0.001 | −0.77 (21 units, p < 0.001) | 5 units: cannot tell | centring not rejected |

The spread of the unit means beyond their noise (C1, exploratory): DerSimonian-Laird τ 0.25 (corals, I² 0.64) and 0.21
(fish, I² 0.67). Against the offline D(u) the correlation is −0.86 (corals) and −0.92 (fish).

**Reading, with its limits.**
- On the corals, the players' own genus ability (the app's δ) is lower on the genera the sheet rates hard: part of the
  hard genera's difficulty sits in the players' δ. By the declared rule, a Q136 rating must use the centred δ, or stay on
  the sheet's chain (today).
- **An association, not a cause.** Who reaches which genus is a confound (strong players meet late genera). And #1310's
  made-up logs showed that rule L2 alone (a miss counted against the genus given) gives this sign when the wrong answers
  fall on the same genera; look-alikes given by mistake do that on the real log. So part or all of it may be the app's
  own bookkeeping, not players' genus-specific skill.
- The fish verdict comes from the second half having 5 units: the rule asks for both halves, and that half cannot tell.
- The player replay passes no starting prior (`p` 0): each δ is that of a player who started at the default (#1310's
  stated limit).

## Check 2B: the step-2 candidates against today's engine

`replay2b.js`, built in this PR. Its rules were committed and pushed (7d0a710ae, 16:58 Manila) before it touched the
real log and before the fit's acceptance verdicts were read (the reference fit's point estimates, with no verdict, had
been printed by then); no rule changed after.
Compute: 5.4 minutes of its own 40, in the machine slot, after 4 minutes waiting for it<!-- effort-ok: compute times -->.

**How it scores.** Each answer is scored before it moves anything, with core.js `scoreAnswer`'s expectation on the
ability the app logged with the answer (so each player's real first-launch prior is in it), the scoring record's memory,
mix-ups, recall and slope; only the photo difficulty differs between the arms. The check: with the difficulty the game
logged, the formula reproduces every logged expected value within its rounding (fish 2,747 of 2,747, corals 1,594 of
1,594). Arm 1 is today's engine: model.js `photoDiff` (run from its source) on core.js `eloReplay` (the engine's own)
over the answers before each one. Arm 2 is the shared chain of the review's section 5 (Kalman split by the variances);
its prior SDs, by the declared rule:

- **Calibration** (expected against right, both arms): both expect fewer right answers than players give, by the same
  amount (fish 0.81 against 0.88 at family rank; corals 0.75 against 0.81), in every format and level band. This comes
  from the shared person side (the logged ability), not from the item side the arms differ on. At fish genus rank arm 2
  expects 0.66 against 0.71 right (arm 1: 0.72), the online rank offset starting at 0 and learning from 160 answers: the
  genus-rank row above.
- **Compression** (the slope of each arm's end-of-log unit offsets on the offline D(u), units with 20 or more answers;
  1 = none): fish (38 units) arm 1 **0.68**, arm 2 **0.98**; corals (43 units) arm 1 **0.66**, arm 2 **0.79**. Today's
  per-unit offsets are shrunk to about two thirds of the scale the offline fit sees; the shared chain keeps more of it.
  Read with care: D(u) comes from the same log (all players), and arm 2 shares the offline model's structure, so a slope
  nearer 1 is partly by construction.
- **Compute**: the fit with its tests and 2C (`run.py`) and 2B together, 36.5 of the 80 minutes their two clocks allowed,
  each run under its own 40<!-- effort-ok: compute times -->.
- **4c on the corals** (p 0.04, 7 of 11 better) is the one exploratory cell under 0.05, among the seven above: by itself
  not a finding (rule L11: exploratory, uncorrected). It points the same way as 2C (rate against the centred δ).

**Limits.**
- **Typed answers are not scored**: 69 fish and 15 coral answers. `prep.js` stores a typed answer's number of choices as
  empty and the scorer drops it, although the declared wording ("2 or more plausible choices") would include them. An
  implementation gap found after the run, not re-run: 69 of 2,816 fish answers.
- Arms 4b and 4c take δ from `checks.js`'s replay, which passes no starting prior (`p` 0), as 2C does; unlike 2C, they
  call it without the fish units' starting units (`treeReplayGen` `from`), which changes the δ of the 160 fish
  genus-rank answers' units until each is answered.
- The compression's D(u) is the reference fit's unit effects, including the fish genus level and the genus rank offset
  that the acceptance test found not estimable: the fish slopes rest partly on values the fit could not measure.
- The arms' shared memory offsets are those of each answer's own prefix; when the trusted players change, the arms' pass
  is made again from the first answer with each answer's own prefix memory, where the sheet's pass would use the new
  trust's memory: a small difference in a term shared by every arm.
- The study's Gibbs sampler was not built: the photo SD and τ stay not estimable, and arm 2 used the v31-equivalent for
  them (declared before).
- Corals: only the answers with a scoring record are scored (1,594 of 3,662); the older ones carry no memory and mix-up
  terms to score them with.
