# An uncertainty model for the abilities, measured on the real log (Q351 step 2, 2026-11-14)

Read-only study for Q351 step 2 (ROADMAP "The expectation's slope, and per-format guess rates"; #651's choices,
decided by Pierre on 2026-11-14): "an uncertainty model (Glicko / Kalman-like) measured on the log first". It changes
nothing in the app. It follows the plan of the diagnosis
([2026-11-06-overconfidence-diagnosis.md](2026-11-06-overconfidence-diagnosis.md) §5.2): each ability gets a variance,
the update is scaled by it, the expectation integrates it, the variances are chosen on the log by leave-one-player-out
likelihood, and a learning drift is tested as a variant of its own.

## Plan, declared before any variant was run

Written and committed before any output of the arms below (rule L11).

**Data.** The sync sheet's Answers tab, read-only: the export of 2026-11-14 (5,668 answers up to 2026-11-13 16:40
Manila), checked against the sheet at 18:45 Manila (same last rows, no answer since). Players are labelled as in
LEARNING.md's 2026-11-09 table and the comeback study (P1-P14; A1, A2 two devices never signed in). Corals are the
primary data (Subject blank or "coral"); fish are exploratory. Nothing is written to the sheet; the copies holding
names stay outside the repository (rule D2).

**What every arm shares.** Each scored answer's difficulty as the game scored it: from its scoring record (photo `d`,
memory `f`, mix-ups `c`, recall `r`, format offset `o`), else, for a row logged before the scorer, recovered from the
logged expected success and ability (the memory and mix-ups the app put in it included), with the format offset of
today's fit (the latest offset logged for that format). The start of a unit never answered (`core.js` `playerStart`,
no first-launch prior: p 0 for everyone), the clamps of `ELO.lo` / `ELO.hi`, and the answer-time weight of a right
answer (`rtWeight`). The prediction is P = g + (1 − g) · R · σ(x), g = 1/k (0 typed), R the recall of a comeback
question, x the arm's log-odds with the format offset.

**Arms.**

- **BASE**: today's rules, `core.js` `scoreAnswer` on `main` at a5b383c3, x = θ − d + offset.
- **DAMP**: BASE's abilities; only the expectation integrates a variance, x = a(v)·(θ − d) + offset,
  a(v) = 1/√(1 + 3v/π²) (Glicko's g). The variance follows the Kalman recursion of GK below, run on BASE's means.
- **GK** (Glicko / simplified Kalman, review §2b): θ = L + δ_unit, the level L and each unit's deviation δ with a mean
  and a variance; before each answer process noise (qL on L, qδ on the unit's δ, per answer, never per day: rule L1);
  the step is the Laplace update of the likelihood above (gradient over Fisher information, Glicko's form), split
  between L and δ in proportion to their variances, so one surprise is counted once; the variances shrink by the same
  update; v = vL + vδ + vD in the damped expectation, vD a photo-difficulty variance. A unit never answered starts with
  δ such that θ is `playerStart` on GK's own level.
- **GKD**: GK plus a learning drift: after each answer on a unit (feedback shown), its δ mean rises by η.

**Grid** (each point a full replay; DAMP and GK 576 points, GKD 1,728): vL0 ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}, vδ0 ∈ {0.1, 0.3,
0.6, 1}, qL ∈ {0, 0.003, 0.01}, qδ ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1}, vD ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6}; GKD also η ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1}. The
point used for a player is the one with the lowest total log-loss on the other players (leave one player out); the
point chosen in each fold is reported.

**Unit of inference**: the player (every coral id with 20 or more scored answers). **One family of six tests, Holm at
α 0.05**, each arm (DAMP, GK, GKD) against BASE:

1. next-answer log-loss: the mean over players of each player's mean difference, exact two-sided sign-flip test over
   the players;
2. the calibration slope on answers whose unit had fewer than 10 earlier answers by the player (m < 10): the slope b of
   P = g + (1 − g)·R·σ(a_format + b·x), one intercept per format (`core.js` `fmtOf`), fitted on each arm's x; the
   difference of the arms' slopes, delete-one-player jackknife standard error, t on players − 1 degrees of freedom.

Expected by the diagnosis: GK lowers the log-loss and moves the m < 10 slope toward 1. No equivalence margin: "not
shown" means not shown, not shown absent.

**Guardrails** (GK and GKD, read with their intervals, not tested): the slope from 10 earlier answers stays near 1 (its
95% jackknife interval meets [0.8, 1.25]); the mean predicted against the mean right per format with 100 or more
answers, the arm's absolute gap no more than BASE's + 0.02; the gap after a miss on a unit with m < 10 (right −
predicted on that unit's next answer, the diagnosis's +0.14), the arm's no larger than BASE's.

**Subsets**, every measure, descriptive: the diagnosis's 2,047 coral answers (logged before the Subject column, up to
2026-11-05) and the 1,261 later ones, the only answers the evidence split was not found on. Cells are given with
their answers and players.

**Checks of the replay before any arm** (they block if they fail): BASE's state after each player against
`core.js` `replayProgress` on the same rows; BASE's ability before each answer against the logged Ability on rows with
a scoring record; BASE on the 2,047 against the diagnosis's replay figures (log-loss 0.4685, slope 0.53, 0.33 under 10
answers, 1.02 from 10; the rules have changed since, so close, not equal).

**Exploratory, outside the family.** Fish: the same arms and measures, LOPO within fish. The simulated world: the same
arms replayed on the app's own answer logs from `tools/learnsim.js` (corals, the four profiles, seeds 1-5, 300
questions each, the item learner and the field learner; it takes the machine slot), where the learner's true chance
is known: log-loss against the answers, mean squared error against the true chance, the slopes at m < 10 and m ≥ 10.
It checks the script and whether the early-slope pattern appears where the truth is known; it cannot size the gain on
real players. Learnsim tier 1 of a changed rule needs the rule in `core.js` and is not part of this read-only step.

**Changed after the plan, before any arm ran.** The second check failed as first run: with p 0 for everyone, BASE's
ability before an answer matched the logged one on 30% of the 1,255 rows with a scoring record, 34% on a unit's first
answer, and the logged one was higher by up to +0.94 on first answers. The cause was the first-launch answer the
plan left out. Each player's coral start record (`reefid-sync-coral-start`, read-only from the Progress data tab, by
label) now gives the prior at each answer (`core.js` `startPriorAt`), in BASE and in GK's start of a new unit. After
that, 59% match within 0.015 (82% of first answers), and the mean absolute gap is 0.037. What is left comes from rows
the device scored under older rules and from A1, a device never signed in whose start record is not in the sheet (p 0
there; its logged ability sits +0.58 above the replay on its 20 answers). Fish use `reefid-sync-fish-start` the same
way.

## Headline

1. **The baseline is reproduced.** On the diagnosis's 2,047 answers, today's rules replayed give a calibration slope
   of 0.28 under 10 earlier answers on the genus (diagnosis 0.33) and 0.96 from 10 (1.02). Log-loss 0.4632 per
   answer (0.4685 then; the rules have changed since).
2. **On the 1,261 coral answers logged since, the pattern is gone.** Under 10 earlier answers the slope is **1.12**
   [0.68, 1.56] (935 answers, 11 players), against 0.28 on the earlier answers. From 10 answers it is 0.58, but on 326
   answers from 6 players its interval runs from −0.35 to 1.51 and says nothing. Over all 3,308 coral answers: 0.61
   under 10, 0.76 from 10. The early-miss gap shrinks the same way: +0.075 on the earlier answers with today's rules,
   +0.010 on the later ones. **The problem the uncertainty model was meant to fix did not recur in the fresh
   answers.**
3. **No uncertainty arm shows a gain on corals.** All six tests of the family are "not shown" (Holm).
   - GK: −0.0011 log-loss per answer (players' mean; 7 of 15 players lower; p 0.70). The early slope moves by +0.05
     (p 0.36).
   - GKD (with the learning drift): −0.0028 (9 of 15, p 0.44), early slope +0.09 (p 0.10).
   - DAMP (only the expectation damped) is worse: +0.0030.
   - Every guardrail holds. GK and GKD do reduce the overall under-prediction per format (BASE predicts 0.796 where
     0.819 were right).
4. **The variances are not identified at this size.** GK's leave-one-player-out choice sat at the edges of the
   declared grid (level variance 2, photo variance 0.6, no genus process noise). A wider grid, run after looking
   (exploratory), moved the choice to the new edges (4-8 and 1-4, 8 different points over 15 folds) and did no better
   (+0.0001). The drift is the one stable choice: η = 0.05 in 11 of 15 folds.
5. **Fish (exploratory, outside the family) show a lead.** GK −0.0084 (7 of 9 players lower, p 0.043 uncorrected), GKD
   −0.0117 (8 of 9, p 0.020 uncorrected). Neither would pass the family's Holm correction. BASE under-predicts fish by
   0.05 (0.829 predicted, 0.879 right), and GK's gain looks mostly like a correction of that mean (inference).
6. **In the simulated world the arms find a gain where the truth is known**: GK −0.013 and GKD −0.027 log-loss per
   answer, 20 of 20 runs (item learner). The squared error against the learner's true chance falls from 0.022 to 0.018
   and 0.014. The simulated error is a lag behind a learner who learns (BASE predicts 0.77 where the true chance is
   0.84). It is not the real log's early-slope pattern: BASE's simulated slope is 0.74 under 10 answers and 0.47 from
   10. So the simulation shows that the script and the arms work, and that a drift helps a learner who keeps learning.
   It cannot say how much real players would gain.

**Reading.** Step 2 measured what it was asked to measure, and at this size the answer is no. A variance model brings
no shown gain on the coral log, and its variances are not identified. The evidence split that motivated it was found
on the first five days and is absent from the eight days after. The log cannot say why the pattern went away: new
players, photo ratings that settled, the players' first week of lectures, or chance on few players. Each fits.

## Limits

- **Few players.** 15 coral players, 5 of them with 200 answers or more, 7 with any genus past 10 answers, and 788 of
  the 925 answers from 10 on coming from three players. A gain of 0.005 per answer would not be detectable. "Not
  shown" is not "shown absent": no equivalence margin was declared (rule L11).
- **The split was found on these data.** The 2,047 earlier answers are the diagnosis's. The 1,261 later ones are the
  only fresh test, and they do not repeat the pattern. That is one fresh look, on 11 players.
- **Same difficulty inputs in every arm.** Every arm reads the difficulty the game logged. In the app, a rule that
  changes the abilities would also change which photos and formats are asked (the aim); a replay cannot show that.
- **No variance per photo.** GK has one shared photo variance (vD), not a variance per photo rating from its weighed
  answers, which the sheet would have to serve. The batch refit (Q351's next part) is where per-photo standard errors
  come from.
- **A Laplace step, not a full filter.** GK ignores the covariance between the level and a genus after an update (the
  simplified Kalman rule). It keeps today's start rule, clamps and answer-time weight, so only the uncertainty changes.
- **A1** has no start record. **Fish** were not in the family, and their format offsets are mostly 0.
- **The simulation** is the app world only (the rated world logs no app answers). Its learners are not real players.

## Reproducing

The scripts (`replay.js`: every arm's replay with the LOPO grid; `stats.py`: the declared measures; `expl.py`: the
exploratory breakdowns) are kept outside the repository with the integrator, with no names in them. They read a
pseudonymised copy of the Answers tab and the start records by label. Replay check: BASE's final state equals `core.js`
`replayProgress` on the same rows for every unit of every player (432 coral and 266 fish records, within rounding).
