## The list and its corrections

The slate lists 39 genera (34 numbered + 5 starred) in 11 families. Only two names are misspelled:

- **7. Tubastrea** → *Tubastraea*
- **15. Pachyserls** → *Pachyseris* (the “i” was printed as an “l”)

All others are correct as written, including *Astreopora*, *Pectinia*, *Plerogyra*, *Mycedium*, *Diploastrea* and *Astrea* (a valid genus since the 2016 Merulinidae revision; not the mollusc *Astraea*). Families below follow current WoRMS classification from memory: check them on [WoRMS](https://www.marinespecies.org) before learning them by heart.

## The 39 genera by family

Grouping by family helps: within a family, look at colony form first, then corallite size and shape. Number = position on the slate (★ = starred genera).

## Look-alike groups

Fifteen groups of look-alikes, each with the feature that decides, checked against the Coral Finder 2022.

| Group | Deciding feature |
| --- | --- |
| *Favites* / *Dipsastraea* / *Astrea* | Mostly shared walls (cerioid), sometimes mixed with separate walls = *Favites*. Separate walls with oval corallites dividing in two = *Dipsastraea*. Separate walls, round crowded corallites of mixed sizes, small ones budding between = *Astrea*. |
| *Favites* / *Goniastrea* | *Goniastrea*: neat corallites and septa with a crown of paliform lobes. *Favites*: more irregular, paliform lobes uncommon. |
| *Platygyra* / *Leptoria* / *Goniastrea* | Equal, evenly spaced septa like a zipper = *Leptoria*. Alternating septa, ragged walls, no paliform lobes = *Platygyra*. Neat septa with paliform lobes, short valleys = *Goniastrea*. |
| *Acropora* / *Anacropora* / *Montipora* | Axial corallite at branch tips = *Acropora*. No axial: thin branches with widely spaced 1-2 mm corallites = *Anacropora*; tiny 0.5-1 mm corallites among bumps and spines = *Montipora*. |
| *Pocillopora* / *Stylophora* / *Seriatopora* | Verrucae = *Pocillopora*. Blunt tips, hooded scaly corallites = *Stylophora*. Very fine branches, corallites in rows = *Seriatopora*. |
| *Goniopora* / *Alveopora* | Count the tentacles: 24 (*Goniopora*) versus 12 (*Alveopora*). |
| *Porites* / *Montipora* | *Porites*: 0.8-1.5 mm corallites with well-defined walls, pitted surface. *Montipora*: 0.5-1 mm, poorly defined, set among bumps, ridges and spines. |
| *Porites* / *Goniopora* | Small-polyp *Goniopora* look like *Porites*, but their polyps are out by day. |
| *Hydnophora* / *Montipora* | Conical monticules lined with costae = *Hydnophora*. Finer ornament with tiny corallites between = *Montipora*. |
| *Astreopora* / *Cyphastrea* | *Cyphastrea*: neat 1-2 mm cones with beaded costae. *Astreopora*: 1.5-3 mm, ragged spinules, mixed heights and directions. |
| *Mycedium* / *Pectinia* / *Merulina* / *Echinopora* | Corallites inclined like noses = *Mycedium*. Large immersed corallites in deep valleys between fluted blades = *Pectinia*. Short valleys fanning out to the margin, herringbone septa = *Merulina*. Round corallites with spiny costae between = *Echinopora*. |
| *Pavona* / *Pachyseris* | Parallel valleys with septo-costae as fine ribs at right angles = *Pachyseris*. 2-4 mm corallites in valleys with sharp ridges, septo-costae flowing between = *Pavona*. |
| Elongate Fungiidae: *Ctenactis* / *Herpolitha* / *Lobactis* | Secondary mouths scattered beside the furrow, small teeth = *Herpolitha*. One furrow, no secondary mouths, large saw-like teeth = *Ctenactis*. Oval, up to 17 cm, tall tentacular lobes = *Lobactis*. |
| Round Fungiidae: *Fungia* / *Danafungia* / *Lithophyllon* / *Heliofungia* / *Halomitra* | Tentacles out by day = *Heliofungia*. Colonial dome up to 50 cm with many mouths = *Halomitra*. Septal teeth large and irregular (>1 mm), disc arched = *Danafungia*; regular and saw-like (\~1 mm) = *Fungia*; fine (<1 mm) and neat = *Lithophyllon*. |
| *Galaxea* / *Tubastraea* / *Duncanopsammia* | Protruding blade-like septa = *Galaxea*. Azooxanthellate, orange or green-black, in shade, smooth tubes = *Tubastraea*. Large raised corallites (\~6 mm) on thick plates (D. peltata), or branching tubes on soft sediment (D. axifuga) = *Duncanopsammia*. |

The round mushroom corals come down to septal tooth size: check close up, or with an IMR colleague.

## IMR lecture groups

The IMR lecture (Lecture 6) sorts the genera into 11 groups by corallite type, not by family. Merulina, Cyphastrea and Halomitra (★) are not covered in the lecture.

| Group | Genera |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Small, raised, separate-walled corallites | *Acropora*, *Anacropora*, *Pocillopora*, *Stylophora* |
| 2. Small, separate-walled, submerged corallites | *Seriatopora* |
| 3. Small, shared-walled, submerged corallites | *Porites* |
| 4. Large, raised, rounded corallites | *Astreopora*, *Duncanopsammia* ★, *Echinopora*, *Galaxea*, *Mycedium*, *Tubastraea*, *Turbinaria* |
| 5. Meandering ridges / valleys | *Leptoria*, *Lobophyllia*, *Pachyseris*, *Pavona*, *Pectinia*, *Platygyra* |
| 6. Polyps extended by day | *Alveopora* ★, *Goniopora*, *Plerogyra* |
| 7. Irregular / angular corallites | *Hydnophora*, *Montipora* |
| 8. Solitary | *Ctenactis*, *Danafungia*, *Fungia*, *Heliofungia*, *Herpolitha*, *Lithophyllon*, *Lobactis* |
| 9. Massive / thick, rounded corallites with separate walls | *Astrea*, *Diploastrea*, *Dipsastraea* |
| 10. Massive / thick, rounded corallites with shared AND separate walls | *Favites* |
| 11. Massive / thick, rounded corallites with shared walls | *Goniastrea* |

### Where the lecture and the Coral Finder differ

- *Plerogyra*: the lecture puts it in Euphylliidae; this doc and current WoRMS use Plerogyridae.
- *Herpolitha*: the lecture lists it as solitary; the Coral Finder describes it as colonial with many mouths along the furrow. Treat it as a free-living, elongate mushroom coral either way.
- *Favites*: the lecture gives it its own group because shared and separate walls can occur in one colony (e.g. F. rotundata).
- *Duncanopsammia*: the species at Dauin is D. peltata (plates), not the branching form shown in the Coral Finder; look under Turbinaria peltata (Coral Finder p50) and on Corals of the World.
- Corals of the World factsheets still use old names for some genera: look up *Dipsastraea* under Favia, *Danafungia* and *Lobactis* under Fungia, and *Astrea* under Astrea or Phymastrea.

These notes come from the slide text and the instructor's speaker notes. Some points are probably made orally during the lecture, or shown only in a photo with no caption, so a gap or a difference here may be settled in class: check it with an IMR instructor.

### What the instructor's notes add

- Order of checks for meanders: paliform lobes first, then the neatness of *Goniastrea*, the zipper of *Leptoria* and the raggedness of *Platygyra* (Coral Finder p18).
- *Leptoria*: valleys so narrow that in photos you see only the ridges.
- *Stylophora*: from afar the skeleton looks smooth, unlike *Pocillopora*.
- *Anacropora*: branch tips can fork.
- *Goniopora*: tentacles typically pulse.
- *Hydnophora*: massive form like a jackfruit, monticules like juicers, corallites in the valleys between them.
- *Dipsastraea* now covers only intra-tentacular, separate-walled corals; *Favites* the extra-tentacular ones with shared or separate walls (*Favites* still under review).
- *Goniastrea retiformis* is often found with *Coelastrea aspera*, which has slightly larger corallites.
- Mnemonics from the lecture: A-"cup"-ora; *Pocillopora* "chicken popcorn" (verrucae = warts); *Stylophora* "style-for-ya"; *Anacropora* "nah, Acropora"; *Seriatopora* "serial black pores"; *Tubastraea* "tubes of a tree"; *Mycedium* "my sea god"; *Pavona* "a star in the pavement"; *Pachyseris* "a series of packed rows"; *Platygyra* "a plate in a gyre" (disorder).

## Coral anatomy terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Septa** | Radial skeletal plates projecting inward from the corallite wall towards its centre. |
| **Costae** | Radial skeletal ribs outside the corallite wall, often continuing the septa. |
| **Wall** (theca) | Skeletal boundary around a corallite; separate or shared with neighbouring corallites. |
| **Coenosteum** | Porous skeleton between corallites; in some taxa it also builds their walls. |
| **Corallite** | The skeleton produced by one polyp. |
| **Oral disc** | Soft tissue around the mouth, inside the ring of tentacles. |
| **Paliform lobes** | Upright projections from the inner edges of the septa; may form a crown around the columella (central skeletal structure). |
| **Mouth** | Opening at the centre of the oral disc, leading to the digestive cavity. |

The oral disc and mouth belong to the living polyp; the other terms describe its skeleton. Sources: [Corals of the World glossary](https://www.coralsoftheworld.org/page/glossary/), [NOAA coral skeleton](https://cdhc.noaa.gov/coral-biology/coral-skeleton/), [NOAA polyp anatomy](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_corals/coral01_intro.html).
